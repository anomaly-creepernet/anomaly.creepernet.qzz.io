Free Email Without NS Delegation

published 2026-08-06

The Problem

Most free email hosting services require you to point your domain nameservers to their servers. This gives them full control over your DNS, which means you lose the ability to use Cloudflare, set up custom records, or manage your own zone. If you want to use your domain for multiple things, a website on one service and email on another, you need your DNS somewhere neutral.

The good news is that email does not require nameserver delegation. Email routing is handled entirely by MX records, which can live at any DNS provider. You can keep Cloudflare as your DNS provider and point your MX records to a mail server somewhere else entirely.

Setting Up alwaysdata Email

Create a free alwaysdata account and add your domain. Go to the Email section and create a mailbox. alwaysdata gives you the mail server hostnames: typically smtp-{account}.alwaysdata.net for SMTP and imap-{account}.alwaysdata.net for IMAP. These work on ports 465 (SMTPS), 587 (STARTTLS), and 993 (IMAPS). The free tier includes 1GB (i think) of storage and infinite mailboxes.

Configuring MX Records at Cloudflare

In your Cloudflare DNS dashboard, add these records with proxy set to DNS only (grey cloud):

Type: MX  Name: @  Content: mx1.alwaysdata.com  Priority: 10  Proxy: OFF
Type: MX  Name: @  Content: mx2.alwaysdata.com  Priority: 20  Proxy: OFF

# SPF record
Type: TXT  Name: @  Content: "v=spf1 include:alwaysdata.com ~all"  Proxy: OFF

# DMARC record
Type: TXT  Name: _dmarc  Content: "v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:postmaster@yourdomain.com"  Proxy: OFF

SPF tells receiving mail servers which IPs are allowed to send email from your domain. The ~all (softfail) means mail from unauthorized servers will be flagged but not rejected. DMARC tells receivers what to do with mail that fails SPF or DKIM checks. Starting with p=none is recommended, it monitors without enforcing.

Intra-Server Email Delivery

An interesting thing about alwaysdata: if you send email from one domain on your account to another domain on the same account, the mail never leaves their infrastructure. For example, sending from user@creepernet.qzz.io to user@creepernet.bond (if both are on the same alwaysdata account) delivers instantly because alwaysdata recognizes both domains locally and skips external routing. This is not something you need to configure, it just works because both mailboxes live on the same physical server.

Email Deliverability and TLD Reputation

The TLD you use actually matters for email. Some free TLDs like .tk and .ml have been so heavily abused by spammers that Gmail and ProtonMail block or spam-filter entire TLDs. If your domain is on a burned TLD, no amount of SPF or DKIM configuration will fix deliverability. The receiving server rejects the mail before it even checks your records.

Newer TLDs like .cyou and .bond currently have clean reputations. Email from these domains arrives normally in Gmail and Proton inboxes. This will likely change if they become popular with spammers, but for now they work well.
